# Worked Example — "data center approvals will be narrowed down to government lands"

Original concept: Michael D. Carter (BitsBeTrippin) · Built with Claude AI assistance · Apache-2.0

> **Everything below is SYNTHETIC.** The transcript, the web pages, the search results, and the model outputs are labeled fixtures in `fixtures/`. No real citation appears here and no real-world outcome is claimed. The point is to show *how the application reasons*, end to end, and the same data drives `server/src/research-pipeline.test.ts`.

<a name="3-validation-plan"></a>
## 3. Validation plan (written before any research)

Generated by the plan stage with the instruction *"Create a research prompt and evaluation plan for checking this prediction. Do not determine the outcome yet."* Stored as **plan v1** with provider, model, template `plan.v1`, and timestamp. The app overwrites the dates block with its own values so the model cannot drift them. Abridged from `fixtures/model-outputs/plan.data-center-approvals.json`:

- **Proposition.** By 2027-11-03, approvals for new data centers will be restricted to government-owned land, with local permit cancellations as the claimed cause.
- **Working definition of "narrowed down to".** Fulfilled if, by the deadline, a clear majority of newly approved projects in the relevant geography sit on federal/state/municipal land, *or* a rule formally restricts approvals to such land. **Geography:** unstated; evaluate the US as the most plausible scope and record that assumption.
- **Would support:** an enacted policy steering siting to public land; siting data showing a majority of new approvals on government land; continued cancellations through the window.
- **Would contradict:** continued approvals of large private-land projects; siting statistics with government land a minority; evidence the shift is driven by power availability or incentives, not cancellations.
- **Partial fulfilment:** premise supported but future claim unsupported → *partially supported* at most; policy announced but not enacted by the deadline → announced, not fulfilled.
- **Queries.** neutral: `data center siting government land 2026`, … · supporting: `federal land data center program approvals 2026`, … · disconfirming: `county approves data center private land 2026`, …
- **Executable research prompt.** Instructs the research agent to use only retrieved sources, record dates/stance/component/action stage per item, separate in-window events from later developments, and never treat missing results as a verdict.

## 5. Assessment — what the model said, and what the app allowed

The (synthetic) assessment model **over-claimed**: overall *supported*, future claim *supported* citing the federal announcement, plus one citation to an evidence id that does not exist. The app-side verdict guard then applied its rules (visible to the user under "Rules applied by the app"):

| Rule | Effect |
|---|---|
| G1 | The invented evidence id was dropped from the citations. |
| G4 | The future-claim component's only supporting item is an *announcement*; announced ≠ implemented → component capped at **partially supported**. |
| G2 | Overall "supported" requires every future-claim component to be supported by in-window evidence → overall downgraded to **partially supported**. |
| G7 | Time status computed by the app: deadline 2027-11-03 → **Deadline pending**. |

Component view:

| Component | Assessment | Why |
|---|---|---|
| premise — local markets keep cancelling permits | **Supported** | Two independent sources document cancellations/revocations in 2026 (the wire copy is syndicated and does not add independence). |
| future_claim — approvals restricted to government land | **Partially supported** (capped) | Only an announced solicitation; state siting data shows private land still dominant in H1 2026. |
| causal_link — cancellations cause the shift | **Insufficient** | No source connects the two. |

**Why cancellations alone prove nothing about the future claim.** The premise and the prediction are different propositions. Evidence that permits are being cancelled establishes the *present-state premise*; it says nothing about *where approvals end up*. Only siting data or an enacted rule can support the future claim, and the only item on that component is an announcement. The guard encodes exactly this: support must attach to the future-claim component, in the window, and be more than proposed/announced.
